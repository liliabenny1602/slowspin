How to play a record

  1. Flat, steady surface. Away from the speakers, so vibrations don't make the stylus skip or hum.
  2. Take the record out carefully. Fingertips on the outer rim, thumb on the label. Never the grooves.
  3. Place it on the platter. Centre hole over the spindle, right side up.
  4. Choose the speed. 33⅓ for most 12-inch LPs, 45 for 7-inch singles. Some 12-inch singles play at 45, so check the label.
  5. Do a dust pass. Drag the carbon fibre brush onto the spinning record.
  6. Start the platter and let it reach full speed.
  7. Lower the needle. Guard off, arm over the outer edge, cue lever down.
  8. Listen. Don't bump the table.
  9. Lift the needle when the side ends. Don't leave it in the run-out groove.
  10. Stop the platter, wait until it has fully stopped, then flip or put the record away.

Built-in sides run about a minute instead of twenty; records pressed from your own songs play at full length. Everything else is to scale: true rotation speeds, true pitch when the speed is wrong.

Record care
  • Hold records by the edges and label. Never touch the playing surface: skin oils attract dust.
  • Back in the sleeve right after playing. Dust settles fast on a record left out.
  • Anti-static inner sleeves (poly-lined or rice paper) beat plain paper, which can scratch and shed fibres.
  • Store upright like books. Never stacked flat, never leaning. Use dividers or bookends.
  • Cool, dry, out of the sun. Keep away from windows, radiators, AC vents and hot cars. In a Dubai summer, never leave records in a car.
  • Brush before every play: a couple of turns, then slowly off toward the edge.
  • Deep clean dirty or second-hand records with vinyl cleaning fluid, wiping along the grooves, never across. Keep the label dry. No household cleaners or alcohol-heavy mixes.
  • Let records dry completely before sleeving or playing.
  • Outer sleeves protect the jacket from dust, ring wear and scuffs.
Turntable care
  • Clean the stylus with a stylus brush, back to front only, never side to side.
  • A stylus lasts roughly 500–1,000 hours depending on type. Worn signs: distortion, harsh "s" sounds, more crackle, skipping. A worn stylus damages records.
  • Set tracking force to the cartridge maker's number. Too light skips and rattles; too heavy wears record and stylus. Match anti-skate to it.
  • Keep the turntable level. Check with a bubble level on the platter.
  • Close the dust cover when the turntable isn't in use.
  • Wipe the platter mat now and then. Dust on the mat ends up on your records.
  • Replace a belt-drive belt every few years, or sooner if the speed drifts or the pitch wobbles.
  • Guard on and tonearm clipped before moving or cleaning the turntable.
Glossary
Platter
The spinning plate the record sits on
Tonearm
The pivoting arm that holds the cartridge over the record
Stylus
The diamond tip that rides in the groove
Cantilever
The thin rod connecting the stylus to the cartridge
Cartridge
Converts the stylus's motion into an electrical signal
Phono preamp
Boosts the weak signal and applies RIAA equalization
RIAA equalization
The standard tone curve that undoes the bass cut and treble boost used when records are made
rpm
Revolutions per minute: 33⅓ for LPs, 45 for singles
Cue lever
A small lever that raises and lowers the tonearm gently
Tracking force
How hard the stylus presses down on the record, in grams
Anti-skate
A setting that stops the tonearm being pulled toward the centre
Run-out groove
The smooth loop at the end of a side, near the label
